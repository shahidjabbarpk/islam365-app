Tahajjud Prayer: Best Time, How to Pray and How to Wake Up for It

2026-11-09

Tahajjud is the voluntary night prayer offered after sleeping and before Fajr. Allah praises those who "forsake their beds, invoking their Lord in fear and hope" (Quran 32:16). The Prophet ﷺ said the best prayer after the obligatory prayers is the prayer at night (Sahih Muslim).

When is the best time?

Tahajjud can be prayed any time after Isha until Fajr begins, ideally after some sleep. The best time is the last third of the night. The Prophet ﷺ said that our Lord descends to the lowest heaven in the last third of every night and says: "Who is calling upon Me that I may answer him? Who is asking of Me that I may give him? Who is seeking My forgiveness that I may forgive him?" (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim)

How to calculate the last third of the night

In Islamic rulings the night runs from Maghrib to Fajr. Take your two times from the Islam 365 Namaz screen:

  1. Find the length of the night: Fajr − Maghrib. Example: Maghrib 5:15 pm, Fajr 5:00 am → 11 hours 45 minutes.
  2. Divide by 3: about 3 hours 55 minutes.
  3. Subtract from Fajr: 5:00 am − 3:55 = about 1:05 am. The last third starts here.

How many rakat?

The Prophet ﷺ said: "The night prayer is two by two" (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). You can pray as few as two rakat. His usual practice was eleven rakat including Witr (Sahih Bukhari). If you have not prayed Witr after Isha, end your Tahajjud with Witr.

Tips to wake up regularly

  • Sleep early, right after Isha if you can.
  • Make the intention before sleeping and read the sleeping sunnahs: wudu, Ayat al-Kursi and the last three surahs.
  • Set an alarm 20 to 30 minutes before Fajr. Even two rakat and a short dua before Fajr counts.
  • Start with one night a week, for example Thursday night, then increase.
