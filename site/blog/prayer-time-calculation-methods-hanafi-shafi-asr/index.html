Have you noticed that two prayer time apps show a different Fajr or Asr time for the same city? Usually neither app is broken. They are simply using different calculation settings. Here is what those settings mean, so you can choose the one your local masjid follows.

Fajr and Isha: the twilight angle

Dhuhr (just after the sun passes its highest point) and Maghrib (sunset) are the same everywhere. Fajr and Isha depend on twilight, the faint light in the sky before sunrise and after sunset. Different scholarly bodies measure this light with a different angle of the sun below the horizon:

A bigger angle means an earlier Fajr and a later Isha. That is why ISNA's 15° gives a later Fajr than the Karachi method.

Asr: Hanafi vs Shafi'i

Asr starts when the shadow of an object reaches a certain length:

  • Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali (standard): when the shadow equals the object's length, plus the shadow it had at noon.
  • Hanafi: when the shadow is twice the object's length, plus the noon shadow.

So Hanafi Asr comes later, often 45 minutes to over an hour later depending on the season. Most masajid in Pakistan, India, Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Turkey follow the Hanafi time.

Tip for high latitudes

In the UK, Scandinavia and Canada in summer, twilight may never fully disappear, so Fajr and Isha by angle become very early or very late. In such places follow the timetable of your local Islamic centre.
