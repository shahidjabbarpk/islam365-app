How to Calculate Zakat: Nisab in Gold and Silver, 2.5% Rule and a Simple Calculator

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam. It purifies wealth and is a right of the poor over the wealth of the rich. Many people delay it simply because the calculation feels confusing. It is actually quite simple.

Who has to pay Zakat?

A sane, adult Muslim whose zakatable wealth is at or above the nisab and stays so for one full lunar (Hijri) year must pay Zakat of 2.5% on that wealth.

What is the nisab?

  • Gold: 87.48 grams (7.5 tola)
  • Silver: 612.36 grams (52.5 tola)

If you only own gold, the gold nisab applies. If you have a mix of cash, savings, silver and business goods, many scholars, especially in the Hanafi school, advise using the silver nisab because it is lower and therefore better for the poor. Check today's market price of gold or silver to convert the nisab into rupees, riyals or dollars.

What counts as zakatable wealth?

  • Cash at home and money in bank accounts
  • Gold and silver, including jewellery (in the Hanafi school)
  • Business stock and goods bought for resale
  • Money others owe you that you expect to get back
  • Shares and investments (according to their nature)

Your house, car, furniture, clothes and tools you use are not zakatable. You may subtract debts that are due now.

The calculation

Zakat = (total zakatable assets − debts due now) × 2.5%

Example: savings of 600,000, gold worth 400,000, a debt of 100,000 due this month. Net = 900,000. If this is above the nisab, Zakat = 900,000 × 0.025 = 22,500.

Many people pay Zakat in Ramadan for the extra reward. Whatever date you choose, keep the same Hijri date every year. For complex cases (business partnerships, pensions, crypto), ask a qualified mufti.
