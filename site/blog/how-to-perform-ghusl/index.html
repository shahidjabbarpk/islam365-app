How to Perform Ghusl: The Obligatory Bath Step by Step

Ghusl is the full ritual bath that removes major impurity (janabah). Allah says: "If you are in a state of janabah, then purify yourselves" (Quran 5:6).

When is ghusl required?

  • After marital relations or a wet dream.
  • After the end of menstruation or postnatal bleeding (nifas).
  • For a new Muslim (recommended or required according to the school).
  • Sunnah: for Jumu'ah, the two Eids, before ihram and at Arafah.

The obligatory parts (Hanafi)

  1. Rinsing the mouth thoroughly.
  2. Rinsing the nose up to the soft bone.
  3. Washing the entire body so that no dry spot remains, including the navel, behind the ears and the roots of the hair.

If any of these is missed, the ghusl is not complete.

The sunnah method

Aisha (RA) described the ghusl of the Prophet ﷺ (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim):

  1. Make the intention and say Bismillah.
  2. Wash both hands.
  3. Wash the private parts and remove any impurity from the body.
  4. Perform a full wudu (some delay washing the feet until the end if standing in water).
  5. Pour water over the head three times, making sure it reaches the roots of the hair.
  6. Pour water over the right side of the body, then the left, until the whole body is washed.
  7. Wash the feet.

Common questions

  • Braided hair: women do not have to undo braids if water reaches the roots (Sahih Muslim).
  • Nail polish: ordinary nail polish blocks water, so it must be removed before ghusl.
  • Wudu after ghusl: a complete ghusl includes wudu; you can pray without a new wudu unless it was broken.
  • Shower is fine: the method above works the same under a shower.
