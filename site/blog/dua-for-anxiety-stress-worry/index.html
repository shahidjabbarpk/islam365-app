Duas for Anxiety, Stress and Worry from the Quran and Sunnah

2026-11-09

Worry, sadness and stress are part of being human. Even the prophets felt grief. Islam does not ask us to pretend everything is fine; it gives us words to turn to Allah with. Allah says: "Verily, with hardship comes ease" (Quran 94:6).

1. Against worry and grief

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَالْبُخْلِ وَالْجُبْنِ، وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ

Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-hammi wal-hazan, wal-'ajzi wal-kasal, wal-bukhli wal-jubn, wa dala'id-dayni wa ghalabatir-rijal.

O Allah, I seek refuge in You from worry and grief, from weakness and laziness, from miserliness and cowardice, from the burden of debt and from being overpowered by people. The Prophet ﷺ used to say this often (Sahih Bukhari).

2. The dua of Yunus (AS)

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

La ilaha illa anta subhanaka inni kuntu minaz-zalimin.

There is no god but You, glory be to You, I was among the wrongdoers. (Quran 21:87). He said it in the darkness of the whale. The Prophet ﷺ said no Muslim calls upon Allah with it about anything except that Allah answers him (Jami' at-Tirmidhi).

3. The dua in distress

La ilaha illallahul-'azimul-halim, la ilaha illallahu rabbul-'arshil-'azim, la ilaha illallahu rabbus-samawati wa rabbul-ardi wa rabbul-'arshil-karim.

There is no god but Allah, the Mighty, the Forbearing. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the mighty Throne. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the heavens, Lord of the earth and Lord of the noble Throne. The Prophet ﷺ used to say this at times of distress (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim).

4. Hasbunallah

Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal-wakil. Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs (Quran 3:173). Ibrahim (AS) said it when thrown into the fire (Sahih Bukhari).

Practical steps alongside dua

  • Guard the five prayers; salah was the Prophet's ﷺ comfort: "Give us rest with it, O Bilal" (Sunan Abu Dawud).
  • Keep morning and evening azkar; they take ten minutes.
  • Talk to someone you trust. Seeking help, including from a doctor or counsellor, does not contradict tawakkul.
  • Sleep, walk and eat well; your body has a right over you (Sahih Bukhari).
