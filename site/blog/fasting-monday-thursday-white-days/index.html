Sunnah Fasts: Mondays, Thursdays and the White Days (Ayyam al-Beed)

2026-11-09

Outside Ramadan, the Prophet ﷺ kept regular voluntary fasts. They are a great way to stay spiritually fit all year and to prepare the body for Ramadan.

Mondays and Thursdays

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Deeds are presented to Allah on Mondays and Thursdays, and I love for my deeds to be presented while I am fasting" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi). When asked about fasting on Monday he said: "That is the day I was born and the day revelation came to me" (Sahih Muslim).

The white days (Ayyam al-Beed)

The Prophet ﷺ advised fasting three days every month, and said that fasting three days each month is like fasting the whole year (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim), since every good deed is rewarded ten times. He recommended the 13th, 14th and 15th of the Hijri month, the nights when the moon is full and bright (Sunan an-Nasa'i, Jami' at-Tirmidhi).

To know these dates you need the Hijri calendar. The Islam 365 home screen shows today's Hijri date, and the Hijri calendar shows the whole month.

Other special fasts

  • Day of Arafah (9 Dhul Hijjah): expiates the sins of the previous and coming year (Sahih Muslim). Not for pilgrims at Arafah.
  • Ashura (10 Muharram): expiates the sins of the previous year (Sahih Muslim). It is sunnah to also fast the 9th.
  • Six days of Shawwal: with Ramadan, like fasting the whole year (Sahih Muslim).
  • Sha'ban: the Prophet ﷺ fasted more in Sha'ban than any other month apart from Ramadan (Sahih Bukhari).

Tips

  • Eat sehri, even a little; there is blessing in it.
  • Start with one Monday a month and build up.
  • If you still owe Ramadan fasts, it is better to make those up first.
