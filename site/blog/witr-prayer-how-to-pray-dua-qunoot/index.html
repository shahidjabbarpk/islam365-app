Witr Prayer: How Many Rakat, When to Pray and Dua Qunoot

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah is Witr (One) and loves the witr (odd), so pray Witr" (Sunan Abu Dawud, Jami' at-Tirmidhi). He never left it, even when travelling. In the Hanafi school Witr is wajib; in the other schools it is a strongly emphasised sunnah.

Time of Witr

From after the Isha prayer until Fajr begins. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Make Witr the last of your prayers at night" (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). If you trust yourself to wake up for Tahajjud, delay it until then; otherwise pray it after Isha.

How many rakat?

  • Hanafi: three rakat with one salam, like Maghrib, except that in the third rakat you recite a surah after Al-Fatiha, then say Allahu Akbar raising your hands, fold them again and recite Dua Qunoot before ruku'.
  • Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali: Witr can be one rakat, or three (often 2 + 1 with a salam in between), or more in odd numbers. Qunoot is recited after ruku' in some cases.

Follow the way of your madhab and local masjid.

Dua Qunoot

In the Hanafi school the commonly recited Qunoot begins: Allahumma inna nasta'inuka wa nastaghfiruka wa nu'minu bika wa natawakkalu 'alayk... ("O Allah, we seek Your help and Your forgiveness, we believe in You and rely on You...").

Another Qunoot taught by the Prophet ﷺ to Hasan (RA) begins: Allahummah-dini fiman hadayt, wa 'afini fiman 'afayt... ("O Allah, guide me among those You have guided, grant me well-being among those You have granted well-being...") (Sunan Abu Dawud, Jami' at-Tirmidhi).

If you have not memorised Qunoot yet, you may recite Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanah... until you learn it.

If you miss Witr

In the Hanafi school a missed Witr is made up (qada) like other wajib prayers. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever sleeps through Witr or forgets it, let him pray it when he remembers" (Sunan Abu Dawud).
