How to Pray on a Plane: Timing, Qibla and Wudu While Flying

Long flights often cross one or more prayer times. Missing a prayer because you are in the air is not necessary. Here is how scholars advise praying on a plane.

1. Try to pray before or after the flight

If the flight is short, pray at the airport before boarding or after landing while the prayer time is still in. Travellers can shorten (qasr) four-rakat prayers, and those following the Shafi'i, Maliki or Hanbali schools may combine Dhuhr with Asr and Maghrib with Isha.

2. Which time applies in the air?

Use the time where the plane actually is. Look out of the window: Maghrib is when the sun has set from your position, Fajr when light appears on the horizon. Some airlines show prayer times on the screen. Prayer apps that use your phone's last location will be wrong mid-flight, so check against the sun.

3. Wudu or tayammum

You can make wudu in the plane toilet using little water. If that is not possible, scholars allow tayammum, and some allow wiping over socks (khuffain) if worn after wudu (in the Hanafi school, only leather or thick socks that meet the conditions).

4. Qibla

Face the Qibla at the start if you can; on many flights the crew can tell you the direction. If facing it is impossible, pray in the direction you can. For a fard prayer, try to face it at least at the beginning.

5. Standing or sitting

Standing is a pillar of the fard prayer, so if there is space near the galley or exit, stand. If you cannot stand safely, pray sitting in your seat and bow your head for ruku and a little lower for sujood. Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity (Quran 2:286).

6. If you could not pray properly

Some scholars advise repeating a prayer prayed sitting without a valid excuse after landing. If in doubt, ask a scholar you trust.
