Zakat al-Fitr (Fitrana): Who Pays, How Much and When

2026-11-10

Ibn Umar (RA) said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ made Zakat al-Fitr obligatory: one sa' of dates or one sa' of barley, on every Muslim, slave or free, male or female, young or old, and he ordered that it be given before the people go out to the Eid prayer" (Sahih Bukhari). Ibn Abbas (RA) said it purifies the fasting person from idle talk and feeds the poor (Sunan Abu Dawud).

Who must pay?

Every Muslim who has food beyond his and his family's needs for the day of Eid (in the Hanafi school: who owns wealth equal to the nisab beyond basic needs). The head of the household pays for himself and his minor children. It is due even for a child born before Fajr on Eid day.

How much?

  • One sa' (about 2.5 to 3 kg) of dates, barley, raisins or a staple food.
  • Hanafi school: half a sa' of wheat (about 1.75 to 2 kg) is sufficient, and it may be given as its value in cash, which is the common practice in Pakistan and India.

Each year the local ulama or Islamic councils announce the minimum amount in local currency based on wheat, dates and other foods. Those who can afford it are encouraged to pay according to the more expensive foods.

When?

  • It must reach the poor before the Eid prayer.
  • Giving it one or two days earlier is fine; many give it during the last ten days of Ramadan so that families can prepare for Eid.
  • Delaying it after the Eid prayer without excuse is sinful, though it must still be paid.

Who receives it?

The poor and needy, the same categories as Zakat. It cannot be given to your own parents, children or spouse.
